Question:

State Henry's law and Raoult's law for a volatile liquid component in a solution. By comparing their mathematical expressions, explain how Raoult's law can be considered a special case of Henry's law.

Marking Scheme & Detailed Solution:
  • **Statement of Henry's Law:** The partial pressure of the gas in vapour phase (p) is proportional to the mole fraction of the gas (x) in the solution, given by p = KH x. 1 Mark
  • **Statement of Raoult's Law:** The vapour pressure of a volatile component in a given solution is directly proportional to its mole fraction (xi), given by pi = xi pi0 (where pi0 is the vapour pressure of pure component). 1 Mark
  • **Comparison and Conclusion:** Comparing both equations (p = KH x and pi = xi pi0), it is observed that both state that the partial pressure of a volatile component is directly proportional to its mole fraction in solution. Raoult's law becomes a special case of Henry's law when the Henry's law constant (KH) becomes equal to the vapour pressure of the pure component (pi0). 1 Mark

(b) Derive the mathematical expression relating the depression in freezing point (Δ T_{f) to the molar mass (M2) of a non-volatile solute. — **[2 Marks]**

(c) An organic substance weighing 2.5 g was dissolved in 80 g of benzene. The depression in freezing point of benzene was found to be 0.45 K. Calculate the molar mass of the solid substance. *(Given: K_{f for benzene = 5.12 K kg mol^{-1)* — **[1 Mark]**

Marking Scheme & Detailed Solution:
  • (a) According to Raoult's law, adding a non-volatile solute lowers the vapour pressure of the solution. Since the solid phase and liquid phase are in dynamic equilibrium at the freezing point, the lower vapour pressure of the solution becomes equal to that of the solid solvent at a lower temperature compared to the pure solvent, resulting in a decrease in the freezing point. 2 Marks
  • (b) **Derivation:**

    • 1. Depression in freezing point (Δ T_{f) for dilute solutions is directly proportional to molality (m):

    \Delta T_{\text{f}} \propto \text{m} \implies \Delta T_{\text{f}} = K_{\text{f}}\text{m}
    — **[1/2 Mark]**

    2. Molality (m) is defined as the number of moles of solute (w2/M2) per kilogram of solvent (w1/1000):

    \text{m} = \frac{w_2 / M_2}{w_1 / 1000} = \frac{w_2 \times 1000}{M_2 \times w_1}
    — **[1/2 Mark]**

    3. Substituting m into the proportionality equation:

    \Delta T_{\text{f}} = \frac{K_{\text{f}} \times w_2 \times 1000}{M_2 \times w_1}
    — **[1/2 Mark]**

    4. Rearranging for molar mass (M2):

    M_2 = \frac{K_{\text{f}} \times w_2 \times 1000}{\Delta T_{\text{f}} \times w_1}
    — **[1/2 Mark]**

  • (c) **Calculation:**

    • M_2 = \frac{5.12\text{ K kg mol}^{-1} \times 2.5\text{ g} \times 1000\text{ g kg}^{-1}}{0.45\text{ K} \times 80\text{ g}} = \frac{12800}{36} = 355.56\text{ g mol}^{-1}
    — **[1 Mark]**

QQ20
Long Answer (LA) / Derivation 5 Marks 🔴 Tier 1
Target Concept: Osmotic pressure relation with molar mass and its experimental superiority over other colligative properties.
Question:

(a) Define osmotic pressure. Why is osmotic pressure considered a colligative property? *(2 Marks)*

(b) Starting from the experimental observation that osmotic pressure (Π) is proportional to the molarity (C) of the solution at temperature T, derive the expression for calculating the molar mass (M2) of a non-volatile solute. State the terms involved in the final expression. *(3 Marks)*

Marking Scheme & Detailed Solution:
  • (a) **Definition:** The excess pressure that must be applied to a solution to prevent osmosis (i.e., to stop the passage of solvent molecules through a semipermeable membrane into the solution). 1 Mark
  • **Colligative Property Reason:** It depends on the number of solute particles (moles) present in the solution and not on their chemical identity. 1 Mark
  • (b) **Derivation:**
  • Experimentally, Π ∝ C × T, where C is molarity.

    • \Pi = C R T
    — **[1/2 Mark]**

  • Since molarity C = \frac{n2{V (where V is volume in litres and n2 is moles of solute), we substitute C:

    • \Pi = \left(\frac{n_2}{V}\right) R T \implies \Pi V = n_2 R T
    — **[1 Mark]**

  • Substituting n2 = \frac{w2{M2 (where w2 is mass of solute in grams and M2 is molar mass):

    • \Pi V = \frac{w_2 R T}{M_2} \implies M_2 = \frac{w_2 R T}{\Pi V}
    — **[1 Mark]**

  • **Nomenclature/Terms:** M2 = molar mass of solute, w2 = given mass of solute, R = gas constant, T = temperature in Kelvin, Π = osmotic pressure, V = volume of solution in litres. 1/2 Mark
